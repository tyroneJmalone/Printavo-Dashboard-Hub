Printavo Hub — Complete Synopsis

A plain-language guide to what the Hub is, how its sections fit together, what each page is intended to accomplish, and where team feedback can have the greatest impact.

In one sentence: The Printavo Hub is Mint Printworks’ internal operating layer around Printavo—bringing information and workflows that were spread across separate apps into one staff-controlled place while leaving Printavo as the primary production system of record.

How the Hub is organized

The left navigation groups work by business purpose. Staff see only modules that are enabled for them; administrators can manage access. Some pages are detail screens reached from a list rather than separate navigation links. The global sync bar communicates how fresh the local Printavo data is and allows a manual refresh.

Team

Tasks (moMINTum)

Shared work management connected to Printavo tasks.

Built

The Tasks link opens the moMINTum artifact, which shares its live task database with the existing standalone app. It supports team lists, assignments, completion, and guarded two-way Printavo task synchronization. It remains a separate app surface linked from the Hub rather than a Hub route.

Feedback focus: Are tasks created in the right place, routed to the right person/list, and visible at the right moment in customer/order workflows?

Data

Backups

Business continuity and portable copies.

Strengthen

The Backups page monitors and triggers the Hub’s full Printavo archive/export process, including historical business records and mirrored files where accessible. It provides a portable recovery asset, but it is not a one-click restore into Printavo. Independent offsite copies, retention, integrity checks, and a restore drill are the highest-priority resilience improvements.

Settings & access

Administration

Controls who can enter and which tools they can use.

Built

Settings manages the staff allowlist, roles, module availability, administrative PIN, and operational defaults such as purchasing ship-to information. Admins can bypass module readiness gating; staff/managers see only enabled tools. Module visibility supports staged rollout, while sensitive data permissions—especially email—are enforced separately.

Customer-facing Reorder Portal

Reorder Portal + Hub inbox

A separate public experience connected to the internal Hub.

New

Customers can search eligible prior orders by email or company name, review order details, select products/quantities, and submit a reorder request. Search results can be shared through a URL that preserves the search. Staff receive and manage submissions in the Hub’s Reorder Requests page.

Current privacy model: a shared URL is a saved public search, not a private authenticated link. Private company links, visitor check-in, and direct invoice creation are intentionally deferred until the current experience is reviewed.

What is intentionally not “finished”

  • Reorder Scan and Color Library: visible placeholders without active pages.
  • Automation ownership: some pollers, emails, PrintNode actions, status rules, and external writes remain guarded by test modes, dry runs, confirmations, or one-environment ownership.
  • AI: designed for reviewable answers, drafts, and suggestions—not broad unsupervised action.
  • Communication integrations: email/correspondence capabilities are evolving; Fireflies and other sources require access/visibility validation first.
  • Backup recovery: exports exist; independent offsite redundancy and practiced restoration need strengthening.

Team feedback worksheet

For each workflow the teammate reviews, capture the answers below. Focus feedback on outcomes rather than feature lists.

1. Job to be doneWhat were you trying to accomplish?
2. Current workaroundHow do you do this today, and how long does it take?
3. FrictionWhere did you hesitate, backtrack, or leave the Hub?
4. Missing contextWhat information did you need but could not see?
5. Next actionWas the correct next step obvious?
6. FrequencyDaily, weekly, monthly, or rare?
7. ImpactDoes improvement save labor, reduce errors, protect revenue, or improve service?
8. ConfidenceWould you trust the data/action enough to use it in real work?

Suggested ranking method

Score each requested improvement from 1–5 on frequency, time saved, risk reduced, revenue/customer impact, and confidence it can be adopted. Subtract 1–5 for implementation complexity. The highest totals should become the next focused refinements; low-frequency “nice to have” ideas stay in the backlog.
