Printavo Hub — Plan, Status & Roadmap

The Hub has moved beyond a collection of ports into a working internal operating platform: shared Printavo data, sales intelligence, customer context, purchasing, production, quoting, shipping, loyalty, communications, AI support, and backups in one staff-controlled system.

Where we are now

The foundation and most core tools are built. The next phase should not be “build everything.” It should be a focused team review: identify the few workflows that save the most time or improve the most decisions, validate them with real staff use, and dial those in before expanding the surface area.

Executive status

Operational foundation

Sync, identity, access & module shell

Staff login, module access, shared Printavo mirror, global sync visibility, route isolation, and a unified navigation system are in place.

Core workflows built

Purchasing, production, quoting & shipping

Vendor ordering, PO tracking, print queues, pricing, shipping estimates, label sorting, and supporting automation have substantial working implementations.

High-value tools evolving

Customer, sales, email & AI intelligence

Client 360, pipeline, correspondence, knowledge, voice profiles, Ask AI, and AI quote drafting are available and should now be shaped by team feedback and measured use.

Operational decisions

Controlled automation and go-live settings

Some write paths and outbound emails intentionally remain guarded, test-routed, dry-run, or single-owner until Tyler explicitly approves live operation.

Recommended focus for the team review

  1. Choose 3–5 “must win” workflows.Ask each teammate where time is lost, where errors recur, and which information is hardest to find. Rank impact by hours saved, risk reduced, revenue influenced, and frequency of use.
  2. Run real workflow validation before adding breadth.Use the Hub on actual day-to-day work, capture friction at the page/action level, and distinguish missing capability from usability polish, data quality, or training.
  3. Dial in the customer and sales workspace.Directory, profiles, pipeline, correspondence, reorder requests, and product curation can become the team’s daily front office if the views, filters, ownership, and next actions match staff habits.
  4. Dial in production and purchasing reliability.Vendor Orders, PO Log, tracking, Print Queue, queue rules, and auto-print have direct operational leverage. Validate handoffs, exception handling, safeguards, and ownership.
  5. Prove AI value with narrow, reviewable tasks.Complete the quote-draft scoring exercise, keep knowledge learning human-approved, and prioritize repeatable answers/drafts over broad autonomous behavior.
  6. Finish operational readiness.Verify offsite backup copies and restoreability, production schema, deployment settings, poller ownership, and test/live email controls before depending on the Hub as critical infrastructure.

Near-term roadmap

Now · Feedback

Structured teammate walkthrough

Invite one representative user. Have them complete common workflows without coaching, record friction and requested outcomes, then rank changes by impact and frequency.

Now · Safety

Operational readiness

Offsite backups, restore confidence, deployment/schema verification, email modes, poller ownership, and restricted real-write testing.

Next · Adoption

Refine the winning modules

Improve navigation, defaults, saved views, filters, ownership, handoffs, and feedback loops in the 3–5 workflows selected by the team.

Next · AI proof

Measured drafting & knowledge

Complete the 50-draft keep/drop review. Let real conversations suggest knowledge only through explicit human approval.

Later · Integrations

Broader communication intelligence

Fireflies visibility audit, additional communication sources, sales follow-up support, and richer cross-channel customer context.

Deferred

Expansion before validation

Private reorder links/check-in, Color Library, standalone Reorder Scan, broad autonomous AI, CFO analysis, and other major additions should wait for focused feedback.

Questions for Tyler after the team review

  • Which three pages would the team use every day?
  • Which workflow currently wastes the most labor?
  • Where would a mistake be most costly?
  • Which data or answer is hardest to find today?
  • Which automation feels safe enough to enable?
  • What should be hidden until it is more mature?
  • Which module needs better defaults, not more features?
  • What result would prove the Hub is paying off?

Non-negotiable operating rules

  • Build and validate incrementally; avoid a big-bang rollout.
  • Keep modules independently tunable so one tool cannot destabilize another.
  • Use Printavo order #22251 only for real-write testing; never use #19004.
  • Non-idempotent vendor/Printavo writes get one attempt; ambiguous results require manual verification.
  • Only one environment may own Printavo ⇄ moMINTum polling.
  • Only one app may own Mint Bucks polling; test emails can consume one-shot ledgers.
  • AI knowledge changes remain draft-first and human-approved.
  • Customer correspondence access must be enforced before retrieval, not only hidden in the UI.
